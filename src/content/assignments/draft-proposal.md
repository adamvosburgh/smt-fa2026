### What

Propose three ideas for your final project. If you have one general idea you want to work with, then three different approaches to it. If you have existing work, or research in another class etc, feel free to bring that into this class.

The final project for *Simulations, Models, Twins* should be three things:

- A simulation, a model, or a twin (a twin is a record, "this is how it is"; a model is a rule, "this is how it works"; a simulation is a run, "this is what happens, if").
- A piece of *representation*, or any visual artifact that is crafting an argument, describing an observed process, building worlds, or testing how a proposal might perform in the "real" world.
- Your piece of representation should not claim any kind of objectivity, and should acknowledge its limitations.


[Final Project](/assignments/final-project/) page has the rest of the semester.

### Requirements

For each of your three ideas, please include:

- **A title and a thesis statement.** Your thesis statement should lay out the project's core argument, position or objective, with the expectation that this will change over time.
- **Expected outcome.** Is this a twin, a simulation or a model? Say which, with a quick description of why. Then, what piece of representation are you hoping to make? Is it a map? A sandbox? A video? A video game? It's ok if this is a guess for now.
- **Datasets.** A list of datasets, with links and descriptions. These must be real datasets, and not a description of a dataset that you assume exists. If you haven't found exactly what you want, link the datasets that are similar to (but not quite) what you would like to find, and we'll talk about it.
- **Mechanics.** If it is something that is not based in data (like a game), a description of the core mechanics and interactions that drive it.
- **References.** A list of reference projects, with a source link or an image, and one or two sentences saying what you like about each. The [projects page](/resources/references/) could be very helpful for this.

I recommend spending some time on the datasets for each idea. It is very easy to pursue a project around the idea of a dataset that may or may not exist. Embrace the constraint of available data.
