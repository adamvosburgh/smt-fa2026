### Due: 9/24

*"Data are always collected for a specific purpose, by a combination of people, technology, money, commerce, and government."* (Kurgan 2013, 35)

### What

Find two datasets about a place you know, join or overlay them in Python, and make one map that says something neither dataset was collected to say.

Write two sentences of gallery text for it.

### Requirements

- The place you select must be somewhere that you have lived and/or spent a significant enough amount of time to know something about lived experience there. It does not have to be New York City.
- Two datasets. At least one must be spatial (a shapefile, GeoJSON, GeoPackage, or a CSV with coordinates); the other can be tabular. At least one must not be from the tutorial.
- Investigate the origins of your two datasets. Some starting points to cover (at a minimum): who made the data? what is/was the intended use(s) of the data? when was the data made? how was it made? This goes in the description.
- Do the work in a notebook, the way [Tutorial 2](/tutorials/02-mapping-where/) did. Include a link to the notebook in your description (in Colab: `Share` > anyone with the link can view).
- Use an appropriate projected coordinate reference system for your place, and say which one.
- A designed composition on a single 16:9 page, with a title, a legend, a scale, a north arrow (north doesn't have to point up...), citations for both datasets, the projection, and your name. Altair gets you most of the way; finish in whatever you like.
- As you design your map choose one of the projects we have looked at thus far in class as a visual precedent, and imitate some aspect of its graphic style. Say which one.

### Either way, submit:

- the map, as a PNG or a single-page PDF
- a two-sentence gallery text
- in the description: the two sources, what each was collected for, what you are using it for instead, the projection, the precedent, and the notebook link

### Submission

`Submit your work` below. It appears under [Student Work](/gallery/#assignment-02). Graded on completion. Your map does not have to provide answers - but can rather ask more precise questions.

### Starting points

- [Columbia Library Geodata Portal](https://geodata.library.columbia.edu)
- [NYC Open Data](https://opendata.cityofnewyork.us/), and the equivalent city or state portal wherever you are
- University mapping and research centers, for example [Eviction Lab](https://evictionlab.org/map/) or the [Beirut Urban Lab](https://www.beiruturbanlab.com/)
- A rule of thumb: think about who would have the motivation and the money to make the dataset you want, then look for that entity.
- The [data sources](/resources/data-sources/) page collects what the tutorials use.
